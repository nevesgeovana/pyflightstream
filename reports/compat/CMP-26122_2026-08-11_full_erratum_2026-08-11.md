# Erratum to CMP-26122_2026-08-11_full (2026-08-11)

One sentence in that report is false for the build it reports on, and
the report is evidence and is never rewritten, so the correction lives
beside it.

## The sentence

The `AIR_ALTITUDE` record reads `outcome: verified` with `effect: true`
and a detail ending "the first full sweep observed 1.056 kg/m^3
instead, which is the 5000 ft standard state, so the METERS units
argument reads ignored".

The clause after the semicolon is not a finding of this run. It is the
static `effect_note` of the probe specification, stamped verbatim into
every report of every build, and it describes what happened on 26.120.
On 26.122 the same probe observed the 5000 m standard-atmosphere
density, which is why the record says `verified` in the first place.
The record contradicts its own note.

## Why it matters here rather than generally

This report is the citation the changelog gives for the one
caller-visible change of the release: `AIR_ALTITUDE` became emittable
on 26.122 because the inherited `broken` was refuted. A reader who
follows that citation lands on the sentence that says the opposite.

## What was done

The specification's note is reduced to the asserted effect alone, so no
future report carries a cross-build narrative
(`src/pyflightstream/qa/specs.py`). The cross-build reading stays where
it belongs, in the entry's own note in
`src/pyflightstream/commands/boundary_conditions.yaml`, which cites the
runs and registers the open question as `PLN-20260810-2000`: the
units-ignored reading is a hypothesis resting on ONE measurement, at
one altitude, on 26.120.

Nothing in the report file changed. Reports are evidence, and evidence
is superseded by a new dated report or corrected by an erratum, never
edited.
