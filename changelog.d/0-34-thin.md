## Added

- The thin blade of a blade mesh (FR-330, its library core): a private module beside the geometry clean, `pyflightstream.workspace._degenerate`, derives the mean surface of a blade from its saved simulation (`.fsm`) or OBJ, the surface midway between its two sides from the root to the tip, with its root (the end nearer the origin of the mesh's coordinates) moved outward along the span by a required root offset so it does not cross the spinner. Vertices an export writes once per side, as an unwelded trailing edge, are merged before the sections are cut. It writes `<stem>_thin_blade.obj` beside the source and its boundary inventory `<stem>_thin_blade.boundaries.toml`, which states the root offset used and, where the source states it, the `[import]` table: the saved simulation's length unit, or the unit and the mesh operations of the OBJ's own sidecar, in order, since the thin blade lies in the blade's frame. It never modifies the source, refuses an existing output unless asked to overwrite, and refuses by name, writing nothing, a mesh it cannot read, a file holding more than one boundary, an OBJ whose sidecar states a CAD or CCS conversion, a blade whose root cannot be told from its tip, a section that is open or whose plane also cuts another closed body in the same boundary, and a blade whose two sides it cannot separate. The command `pyfs-matrix degenerate` that calls it lands with the command-line family of the parser.
